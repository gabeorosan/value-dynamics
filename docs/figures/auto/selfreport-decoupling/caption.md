# Selection moves the behavior; the OLMo risk model's self-description barely moves

Selection loops move the OLMo risk model's (K2 chassis) risk behavior massively while its stated risk tolerance barely follows. Each dot in panel A is one selection-loop rollout, plotted as change in behavior (P(choose the riskier option) on held-out EV-neutral A/B gambles, final round minus round 0) against change in stated tolerance (P(describes itself as the risk-tolerant one) on an order-balanced forced description choice, same checkpoints). Both axes share one scale, so the dashed diagonal is perfect one-for-one tracking; the fitted line through the 34 rollouts whose behavior moved by at least 0.15 has slope 0.05 (r = 0.78) — the direction follows, at one-twentieth the size. Extremes: an oracle-judge hold reverses behavior 0.92 to 0.09 while stated tolerance goes 0.33 to 0.31 (seed 21), and a head-to-head duel invasion rails behavior 0.21 to 1.00 while stated goes 0.29 to 0.33 (seed 54). Panel B shows the per-group mean tracking ratio (stated change divided by behavior change, moved rollouts only) against the same perfect-tracking reference: no condition group exceeds +0.14, most sit near +0.03. Across all 46 rollouts, the mean absolute behavior-minus-stated gap widens from 0.167 at round 0 to 0.341 at the final round; final behavior spans 0.00 to 1.00 while final stated tolerance stays between 0.25 and 0.39.

Source data: `experiments/selfreport_calibration_k2.json` (46 K2-chassis rollouts; per-rollout `traj`, `sr`, `d_traj`, `d_sr`, `tracking_ratio`, and the `aggregates` block).
