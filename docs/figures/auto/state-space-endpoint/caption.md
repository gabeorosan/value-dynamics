**Where will the run end up? A three-variable loop model answers with calibrated intervals.** A 3-state model of the self-training loop — pool mean risk p, judge-candidate alignment rho (correlation of the judge's scores with candidate risk), and candidate supply sigma (candidate risk spread) — is fit as three linear equations on round transitions and rolled forward as 2000 Monte-Carlo paths from each run's round-1 state, leave-one-run-out. Main panel: one row per held-out run (13 OLMo K2 runs, 12 Qwen K1 runs; blue = self-judge, green = frozen judges), showing the 80% predicted interval, its predictive median (tick), and the true endpoint (dot) on the risk coordinate (fraction of the model's answers on 12 held-out gamble questions that pick the gamble). 23 of 25 true endpoints land inside their 80% intervals (92% empirical on each grid); the one structural failure is the frozen base-model judge seed 5 run, whose round-1 state (p 0.17, rho 0.01, sigma 0.20) was indistinguishable from settled runs before alignment bloomed mid-run from sampling luck — truth 0.653 at PIT 0.997, priced only as tail mass. Right panel: median-forecast MAE beats the gap-autoregression and persistence baselines on OLMo (0.126 vs 0.136 vs 0.167) and correctly ties persistence on Qwen (0.120 vs 0.123), where the fan is training instability and the honest forecast is a wide-but-calibrated interval; the fitted OLMo coefficients show the runaway loop directly — the same product rho·sigma moves the pool at slope 1.21 and feeds alignment back at slope 1.22.
